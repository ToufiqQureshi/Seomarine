**Short answer:** Track organic discovery into your CRM and connect landing pages, leads, qualified opportunities, and closed revenue over a sales cycle that matches your business. Report both first-touch and assisted influence where possible, define the attribution model, and separate known revenue from estimated contribution. SEO ROI cannot be proven with traffic alone.

## Why this problem happens

A B2B buyer may read several pages, return direct, attend a demo, and close months later. Last-click analytics can miss earlier organic discovery; first-click can over-credit it. Forms may be duplicated, phone calls untracked, and lead quality may differ sharply by landing page.

## Check these signals before changing the site

1. Agree on a sales cycle and outcomes with sales: lead, qualified lead, opportunity, closed-won revenue, renewal, or pipeline. Use the stage names your CRM actually records.
2. Capture original landing page, source/medium, campaign, timestamp, and consent-compliant identifiers in the CRM. Keep direct return visits from overwriting first-touch data.
3. Test form and call tracking across devices, consent states, redirects, and thank-you flows. Check for duplicate leads and spam.
4. Compare organic leads by page and query theme to qualification rate, close rate, deal value, and time-to-close. Small samples need cautious interpretation.

## Fix it in a safe order

### 1. Build a joined funnel

Connect sessions or leads to CRM records with stable IDs where consent and systems allow. Preserve both first-touch and recent-touch source so the team can see the path without pretending one model is perfect.

### 2. Use cohorts

Group leads by the month or quarter of first organic discovery, then follow them through the normal sales window. Compare mature cohorts rather than judging a recent month before deals have time to close.

### 3. Report revenue and influence separately

Show confirmed organic-sourced closed revenue separately from assisted pipeline or modeled influence. State the attribution model and what it leaves out.

### 4. Make investment decisions from marginal evidence

Compare incremental SEO cost with qualified pipeline and closed revenue over time. Consider seasonality, brand demand, paid campaigns, and sales changes before claiming causation.

## How to tell the fix worked

Review CRM source completeness, duplicate rates, and time-to-close quarterly. Keep definitions stable and show cohort maturity; if attribution coverage is weak, report the gap rather than manufacturing an ROI percentage.

Keep a simple record for each affected URL: the problem, evidence, change, owner, and review date. This is useful when several teams touch the CMS, hosting, analytics, and content. If the same symptom returns, the record helps you spot the shared template or configuration instead of repeating one-off repairs.

## Mistakes that make this worse

Do not divide all revenue by SEO spend and label it incremental SEO ROI. Do not claim an organic lead caused a sale just because the last session was organic.

Before changing a live site, save the current configuration and test the change on a small set of representative URLs. If you are unsure whether an action removes content, changes access, or affects checkout, get the developer or site owner to review it first. Fixing the visible symptom while breaking a related page can cost more than the original search issue.

## FAQ

**What is a good SEO ROI for a long sales cycle?**

There is no universal benchmark. Set a baseline for your industry, margin, sales cycle, and measurement quality, then assess mature cohorts.
