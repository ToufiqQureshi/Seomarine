**Short answer:** Start keyword research with the services you actually sell, the questions customers ask, and the areas you genuinely serve. Check how Google interprets each search, group similar questions by intent, and map each group to one helpful page.

![A local keyword map combining service, place and buyer question into a relevant page](/site/blog/local-keyword-map.svg)

## Why “more keywords” is not the goal

A list of hundreds of terms is not useful if the business cannot serve those searchers. A local plumber should prioritise searches about real services and areas, not chase a broad phrase like “plumbing” with no clear buying intent.

There is also a difference between learning and buying searches:

| Search | Likely intent | Useful page |
| --- | --- | --- |
| how to stop a tap leaking | Learn or solve a small problem | Clear guide, with service option if needed |
| emergency plumber in Surat | Find help now | Local service page and contact action |
| plumber cost to replace a tap | Compare price | Pricing explanation or estimate page |

Search intent is a useful interpretation, not a guarantee of what every person wants.

## A simple research process

### 1. List the services you can deliver well

Write down the actual service names, common variations, urgent problems and high-value jobs. Ask staff to note phrases customers use on calls or WhatsApp. Do not include a service just to attract visits.

### 2. Add real locations

List the towns, neighbourhoods or districts you genuinely serve. Use Google Business Profile and your website to state this accurately. Do not make a separate page for every place if the content would be nearly identical.

### 3. Check live search results

Search the phrase and inspect the results. Are they service pages, maps, directories, how-to guides or comparison pages? If the results mostly show guides, a service page alone may not answer the question. If maps dominate, make sure your business information and eligibility are in order.

Autocomplete and related searches can reveal wording, but they do not provide verified search volume. Use Search Console for queries that already produce impressions on your site. Use a keyword tool if you need volume and competition estimates, and treat its numbers as estimates.

### 4. Group by intent and choose a page

Put close variations with the same purpose together. Do not create separate pages for “AC repair Surat” and “air conditioner repair in Surat” if they need the same answer. Use one strong page and write naturally.

### 5. Check business value

Ask: Can we serve this searcher? Is the job profitable? Does the query suggest urgency, comparison or learning? A smaller number of highly relevant enquiries can matter more than broad traffic.

## A worked example

A Pune physiotherapy clinic may group “sports injury physiotherapist Pune” and “physio for running knee pain Pune” under a specialist service page if the clinic truly offers that care. A separate guide could answer “when should I see a physiotherapist for knee pain?” with careful, medically responsible information. The service page should explain the clinic’s process; the guide should explain the question. A generic “Pune best physiotherapy” page with no local proof adds little.

## Build a basic keyword map

Use a simple sheet with columns for query, intent, service, place, target URL, proof needed, and next action. Add Search Console impressions when available. Review it when services or service areas change.

## Research India and the United States separately

The same service can be searched in different words by market. A U.S. homeowner may add a city, ZIP code or “near me”; a customer in India may include a locality, landmark or language variation. These are examples to investigate, not assumptions about every customer. Use the country and city settings in your keyword tool, then check the live results and your Search Console data.

Keep separate rows for each target market so you do not combine different intent or currencies into one opportunity. Make sure the company can actually deliver there. If the business serves one country only, do not create international pages that imply otherwise.

Finally, ask the staff who answer enquiries which phrase the customer used. Search tools show estimates; real calls tell you whether the wording attracts a buyer the business can help.

## Choose topics using business fit

Score each keyword group from one to three on three questions: can we deliver this service, does the search show a clear customer need, and can the page provide proof or a useful next step? A phrase with a modest estimate but strong fit may be more valuable than a broad, high-volume term. Keep the reasoning beside the keyword so the next person understands why the page exists. Review the map when the team changes its services, pricing or service area; old keyword lists can keep sending visitors to offers the business no longer provides.

## A keyword map you can actually maintain

Use one row per distinct customer need, not one row for every spelling variation:

| Customer need | Example search | Intent | Best page | Proof or next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Find a nearby provider | “emergency plumber in Andheri” | Call now | Emergency plumbing page | Service hours, response area, phone |
| Compare a service | “water heater repair cost Mumbai” | Evaluate | Repair page or pricing guide | What changes the quote |
| Understand a problem | “water heater leaking what to do” | Learn, possibly urgent | Helpful safety guide | Safe first step and service link |

The searches are examples, not volume claims. Validate them with Search Console, customer calls, sales conversations and the live results. Keep location wording honest: a page for a town you do not serve will frustrate visitors and can create doorway-style content.

### Decide whether to create a page

Create a separate page only when the search has a meaningfully different need and you can provide distinct, useful information. If two phrases lead to the same customer and same result page, improve one good page instead of splitting its authority. Add the target query naturally to a title or heading only where it helps people understand the page.

For India, record language and city separately. “SEO agency in Pune” and “SEO कंपनी Pune” may reflect different language preferences; do not assume the same page serves both equally. Ask customers which language they use and check the actual result page before choosing.

## FAQ

**Do I need a separate page for every keyword?**

No. One page can answer several closely related searches when they share the same purpose.

**Should I use a city name in every heading?**

Use it where it helps people understand the location. Repeating it unnaturally makes the page harder to read.

**Can I trust free search-volume estimates?**

Treat estimates as directional. Confirm demand with your own Search Console impressions and real enquiries over time.
