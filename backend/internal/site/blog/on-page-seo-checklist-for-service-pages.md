**Short answer:** On-page SEO means improving the visible content and page elements so people and search engines can understand the page. Start with one clear service, a descriptive title and heading, helpful sections, useful links, accessible images and a working contact action.

![An on-page SEO checklist covering title, headings, helpful content, internal links and images](/site/blog/on-page-seo-checklist.svg)

## 1. Match the page to one real purpose

Write down the customer and task this page serves. A page for emergency plumbing should not also try to be a broad plumbing encyclopedia. Put the important service phrase naturally in the title, main heading and first section, but use the words customers need rather than repeating the same phrase.

## 2. Write a descriptive title and heading

The browser title should describe the page accurately and be different from other page titles on the site. The main heading should help a person confirm they are in the right place. Google may generate title links and snippets differently from what you set, so write for accuracy and clarity rather than relying on a character formula.

## 3. Answer the practical questions

Explain service scope, price factors, process, service area, timing, qualifications and next step where relevant. Use short sections and specific examples. Avoid vague filler written to reach a word count.

## 4. Use headings and links to guide people

Organise long pages with descriptive subheadings. Link to relevant services, case examples, contact details and guides. Use link text that describes the destination; “click here” does not tell a reader what they will find.

## 5. Give images context

Use images that help customers understand the service, not just decorative stock photos. Add concise alt text describing the useful information in the image. Compress images and set dimensions so they load well and do not make the layout jump.

## 6. Check the mobile page

Open the page on a real phone. Test menus, call links, buttons, forms and page speed. If a pop-up covers the main content or the form is difficult to use, fix that before chasing tiny keyword adjustments.

## 7. Measure a result

Use Search Console for queries, pages, clicks and impressions. Use analytics or your CRM for calls and enquiries. Record the page, change and date, then compare after Google has had time to recrawl.

## Common on-page problems

- Several pages use the same title and do not say which service they cover.
- A heading promises a guide but the page is a sales pitch.
- Images have filenames but no useful alt text.
- The page has no links from other parts of the site.
- Keywords are repeated in every heading, making the text awkward.
- The page looks good on desktop but hides the call button on mobile.

## Test one page from search to contact

Use a phone and a private browser window. Search for a realistic service phrase, open the result, and follow the page to the action. Note whether the title matches the page, the first screen explains the service, the call button works and the form confirms a successful submission.

Then open the HTML or your SEO tool to check the page title, canonical URL and index setting. Confirm the image is not huge, the alt text describes the image, and the page links to a useful related guide. For a CMS, check the template too: a shared template error can affect dozens of pages.

Do not add structured data just to fill a score. Use markup only when it describes information visible on the page and follows current guidelines. Test changes on a sample before applying them site-wide.

## Review the page as a customer would

Ask someone who does not know the business to spend two minutes on the page. Ask them to tell you the service, area, price process and next step. If they need to hunt for the phone number or cannot tell whether the business handles their problem, the page needs a clearer opening.

Then compare the search result with the page. A title that says “emergency repair” should lead to a page that explains how to request urgent work. A result that promises a free estimate should not open a page that hides the estimate process. This match builds trust and helps customers self-select.

A useful on-page review is not a score chase. It is a short check of clarity, evidence, navigation, accessibility and measurement on the pages that matter most.

## FAQ

**How many times should I use a keyword?**

There is no useful fixed count. Use it where it helps describe the page and answer the reader’s question.

**Does adding schema automatically improve ranking?**

No. Structured data can help search engines understand eligible content, but it does not guarantee a rich result or higher ranking.
