**Short answer:** If your Google Business Profile is not showing, first confirm that the business is eligible and verified, then check for account notices, duplicate profiles and missing or inaccurate details. Local visibility also depends on relevance, distance and prominence; no setting can guarantee a particular map position.

![A Google Business Profile troubleshooting checklist for eligibility, verification, details and location](/site/blog/business-profile-not-showing.svg)

## Make sure the business is eligible

Google Business Profiles are for businesses that meet Google’s eligibility rules. A business that meets customers in person can usually have a profile; an online-only business generally is not eligible. Service-area businesses should follow Google’s address and service-area rules rather than using a virtual office or a location where they do not meet customers.

Read the current rules before creating another profile. Duplicate listings can confuse customers and may cause more problems.

## Check verification and account status

Sign in to the Google account that owns the profile. Look for a verification prompt, suspension notice or request to confirm details. If verification is pending, follow the method Google offers for that business. Do not repeatedly make new profiles to get around a review.

Keep access with the business owner. If an agency helps, add it through the profile’s user controls rather than sharing passwords.

## Improve completeness and relevance

Check the exact business name, primary category, address or service area, phone number, hours and services. Use the real-world name that customers see, not a string of extra keywords. Choose a category that best describes the main business and add other accurate categories only where appropriate.

Add a useful description and recent photos. Details should match the website and other trusted listings. If the profile says one thing and the site says another, fix the source information.

## Understand what location can and cannot change

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. You can improve accuracy and relevance; you cannot change how close a searcher is to your business. A profile may appear for one person and not another because results can vary by location and query.

Check a few realistic searches from the areas you serve, but do not treat an incognito search as a perfect ranking report. Record the query, place and date so comparisons are fair.

## Review and duplicates

Search for the business name and address. If there are duplicate profiles, use Google’s suggested process to report or resolve them. If the business moved or changed its name, update the existing profile where possible and keep the website, directories and profile consistent.

## When to ask for support

If the profile is suspended, read the notice and appeal using accurate documents. Do not create replacement listings while an appeal is active. If an agency is involved, ask for a written list of changes, proof of verification and confirmation that you retain owner access.

## A practical check for an agency or owner

Write down the account that owns the profile, the exact business name, category, address or service area, phone and website. Compare those details with the official site and any public directory that matters. Take screenshots before editing so you can explain what changed if the profile is reviewed.

If the issue is a suspension, use the appeal route and evidence that matches the real business. Do not add a keyword to the name, switch to a virtual address or create new listings to try to bypass the review. If the profile is simply not visible for a broad phrase, check relevance and distance before making changes. A profile cannot appear near every searcher when the business is far away.

For a multi-location business, make sure each location has a genuine eligible place and distinct accurate details. Do not duplicate one branch to represent another area.

## Check if visibility is local to the searcher

Ask a colleague in the service area to search for the business and record the phrase and approximate location. A business in Denver may not appear the same way for someone searching from a suburb 40 miles away. Compare relevant searches from the actual places you serve, and do not use rank-checking screenshots without their location and date.

If the profile is verified and accurate but appears for the business name only, improve the website and profile details that prove relevance to the service. Add clear service pages, correct categories and real photos. Do not add unrelated categories or keywords to the business name to force visibility.

When speaking to an agency, ask them to separate profile status, local relevance and distance. These are different causes and need different actions.

## FAQ

**Can I add keywords to my business name to rank higher?**

Use the real-world business name. Extra keywords that are not part of the actual name can violate profile guidelines and risk suspension.

**Why does my profile show on Maps but not for a search?**

The query, location, relevance and prominence all affect local results. Being visible for your business name does not mean the profile will show for every service search.

**Should I make a second profile for another service?**

Only when the business genuinely meets Google’s eligibility requirements for a separate profile. Do not create duplicates to target extra keywords.
