**Short answer:** Google Search Console shows how your pages appear in Google Search: the queries people use, impressions, clicks, click-through rate and average position. Start with the Performance report and Page Indexing report. Use them to choose one practical improvement at a time.

![A Search Console workflow from query and page data to one improvement and a follow-up check](/site/blog/search-console-workflow.svg)

## What Search Console can and cannot tell you

Search Console helps you understand Google Search visibility and technical indexing issues. It is not a full website analytics tool: it does not show every visitor action, phone call or sale. Use analytics or your CRM to understand what visitors did after they arrived.

Before reading reports, verify the correct site property. A domain property is useful when you need to include subdomains and protocols. Give access only to people who need it, and keep an owner account that your business controls.

## Start with three reports

### 1. Performance: queries and pages

Choose a date range and review clicks, impressions, click-through rate and average position. Switch between the Queries and Pages tabs. Look for:

- Queries with impressions but few clicks: does the page answer that search clearly?
- Pages getting clicks for an unexpected query: is the page’s purpose unclear?
- A useful query ranking around positions 5–20: could a better section or internal link help?
- A sudden change: compare the same dates and check seasonality before assuming a penalty.

Average position is an average across searches, locations and devices. It is not a promise that every visitor sees your page at that exact rank.

### 2. Page Indexing: pages Google may not show

Review the reasons pages are not indexed. Separate pages you deliberately exclude, such as private previews or filtered URLs, from important pages you want in Search. Inspect a sample URL before changing templates or site-wide settings.

### 3. Sitemaps: discovery, not a ranking button

Submit the sitemap that your site actually serves and check for read errors. Keep only preferred, canonical URLs in it. A submitted sitemap can help Google discover URLs; it does not force indexing.

## Turn reports into a weekly routine

Pick one meaningful question:

1. Which service page earned relevant impressions but few clicks?
2. Which useful page is not indexed?
3. Which page lost clicks, and did impressions also fall?
4. Did a recent fix change clicks or enquiries?

Write down the URL, the evidence, the change and the date. Then allow enough time for Search Console to collect data. Avoid changing titles every day: frequent edits make it harder to learn what helped.

## Example: an electrician in Jaipur

Suppose the Queries report shows impressions for “electrician for inverter repair Jaipur,” while the website only has a general “Our Services” page. The report does not prove that a new page will rank. It does show a real search question worth checking. The owner can decide whether inverter repair is a service they provide, create a helpful section or page with service details, and track whether relevant impressions and enquiries follow.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Treating every excluded URL as an error.
- Treating an average position as a fixed rank.
- Comparing different seasons as if demand were unchanged.
- Submitting a sitemap repeatedly instead of fixing the page.
- Measuring clicks but never connecting them to leads or sales.

## Use comparisons that answer a business question

When traffic changes, compare the same length of time and look at both clicks and impressions. If impressions stay similar but clicks fall, inspect the queries and the result page layout. If impressions fall, check for a change in demand, indexing or relevance. If clicks rise but leads do not, connect the landing page to analytics or the enquiry records.

For a new business, begin with branded searches, then review service and location queries. For an established company, compare important pages and groups of related queries. Avoid reporting one average position without saying the date range, country, device and page group. A position is an aggregate view; customers can see different results.

Share access with a separate account rather than passing around the business owner's password. Remove access when a contractor no longer needs it, and keep a record of who can change site settings.

## Make a simple action log

A small spreadsheet is enough: record the date range, country, page, query pattern, clicks, impressions, issue, change and review date. Keep the first version of a title or page copy so you can compare it later. If the page has very few impressions, wait for more data or use customer calls and sales questions as evidence. Avoid treating a tiny change in average position as a business result. The purpose of this log is to connect one observed problem to one change, then check whether qualified visits or enquiries improve.

## FAQ

**Is Google Search Console free?**

Yes. It is a free Google service for site owners and people they grant access to.

**Does it show every search?**

No. Some queries are omitted for privacy, and reporting is not a complete record of all searches or visits.

**Is Search Console enough to measure SEO?**

It is essential for Search performance and indexing. Pair it with analytics and business records to understand leads and revenue.
