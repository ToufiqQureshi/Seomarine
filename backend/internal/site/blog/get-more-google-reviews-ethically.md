**Short answer:** Ask every customer for honest feedback soon after a real service, make the link easy to open, and never offer discounts or gifts for positive reviews. Reply to feedback with care, protect customer privacy and use complaints to improve the service.

![A fair review process that asks every customer, protects choice and replies with care](/site/blog/ethical-review-process.svg)

## Why reviews matter to a local business

Reviews help people decide whether to contact a business. Google also says more reviews and positive ratings can help local ranking, but this does not mean a specific number guarantees a map position. Relevance and distance matter too, and a business cannot control what customers write.

A good review process makes it easy to share an honest experience without pressuring people or selecting only those who seem happy.

## A simple process customers can trust

### 1. Ask after the service is complete

Choose a natural point: after a repair, delivery, stay or appointment. Use a short message that explains why you are asking and gives the customer a choice.

Example:

> Thank you for choosing Greenway Dental. If you would like to share an honest review of your visit, you can use this link: [review link]. Your feedback helps us improve. No pressure either way.

### 2. Make the link easy to use

Create the review link from the Business Profile, then place it in a follow-up email, receipt or QR code at the counter. Check that it opens the correct profile on a phone.

### 3. Ask consistently, not selectively

Ask customers in the same appropriate way. Do not ask only people who said they were happy, and do not ask staff, family members or vendors to pretend to be customers.

### 4. Never trade rewards for reviews

Do not offer a discount, gift, contest entry or refund in exchange for a review. Do not ask for a particular rating or wording. Incentives can undermine trust and violate platform rules.

### 5. Reply without exposing private information

Thank the reviewer for specific feedback. For a complaint, acknowledge the concern and offer a private way to continue. Do not mention appointments, health details, addresses, order numbers or other private information in a public reply.

## Turn feedback into service improvements

Review themes can point to real problems: missed appointment windows, unclear pricing, hard-to-find directions or slow support. Track repeated themes in a simple sheet. Fix the process when possible and explain the change to staff.

Do not use AI or a template to publish a reply that sounds dismissive. Draft it, check the facts, and make it sound like the person responsible for the business.

## What to do if a review is unfair or fake

Do not argue publicly or retaliate. If a review violates Google’s content policies, flag it through the official tool and keep evidence. A negative opinion by itself is not proof that a review is fake. Respond calmly while the platform reviews it.

## Make the process easy for the team

Create one short request for each appropriate channel: a message after a completed service, a line in a receipt, and a QR code at the counter. Keep the invitation optional and ask for honest feedback. Make sure the link leads to the correct business profile.

Set a reminder for staff to ask consistently, not only when someone praises the business. Review new feedback once or twice a week and route service complaints to the right person. A public response should not become an argument or a customer support ticket with private details.

If the business uses a review management service, read how it selects recipients and handles negative feedback. Do not use a system that sends only happy customers to Google and diverts unhappy customers to a private form. A fair process asks for honest feedback without controlling what the customer says.

## Write replies that sound like a real person

A useful reply mentions the feedback, thanks the customer and explains a next step when needed. It does not argue about every detail or repeat the same canned sentence under every review. If the reviewer describes a specific service, acknowledge it only if you can safely confirm the details.

For a negative review, invite the person to contact a private support channel. Do not ask them to edit or remove the review in exchange for a refund or benefit. Resolve the service issue because it is the right thing to do, not as a bargain for a better rating.

Review replies are public and can be read by future customers. Treat them as part of customer service, not a keyword placement opportunity.

## FAQ

**Can I ask customers to leave a five-star review?**

Ask for an honest review. Do not ask them to choose a particular rating or say specific things.

**Can I give a discount to thank someone for reviewing?**

Do not offer a reward in exchange for a review. Keep review requests separate from sales promotions.

**Should I reply to every review?**

A thoughtful reply can show that the business listens. Prioritise useful, human responses and never disclose private information.
