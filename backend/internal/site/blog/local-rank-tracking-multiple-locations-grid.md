**Short answer:** Track local visibility from consistent points inside the real service area and separate map results from organic website rankings. For multi-location businesses, give each eligible location its own profile, landing page, and measurement set. A single city-centre search cannot describe how visible a business is across an entire metro area.

![A practical troubleshooting flow for how to track local rankings across multiple cities and locations](/site/blog/local-rank-tracking-multiple-locations-grid.svg)

## Why this problem happens

Map results can vary by proximity, relevance, and prominence. A user several miles away may see different businesses than someone near the address. Rank tools sample chosen coordinates; they do not report a universal map-pack position. Fake locations or repetitive doorway pages can create policy and quality problems.

## Check these signals before changing the site

1. List real staffed locations and service areas. Confirm which businesses are eligible for a Google Business Profile and whether addresses should be displayed or hidden under current guidelines.
2. Separate queries with local intent (“near me,” city modifiers) from broad organic terms. Record whether each metric refers to Maps, local pack, or blue links.
3. Choose repeatable grid points inside the legitimate area and keep spacing, device, query wording, and schedule stable.
4. Check each profile’s category, name, address/service area, hours, phone, landing page, and duplicate listings. Do not create a profile for a city with no eligible business.

## Fix it in a safe order

### 1. Create location-specific measurement sets

For each genuine location, choose core service queries and representative points customers can actually reach. Keep branded and non-branded queries separate.

### 2. Report reach, not a single rank

Show the share of sampled points where the business appears, median position when present, and locations with no visibility. Explain the grid and avoid claiming it covers every searcher.

### 3. Improve the location’s real-world signals

Keep profile data accurate, earn authentic reviews, build useful local pages with proof of service, and make contact and booking information consistent. Do not stuff city names into every page.

### 4. Tie visibility to calls and bookings

Track calls, direction requests, appointments, and qualified leads by location where possible. Compare profile data with CRM/phone logs and note limitations.

## How to tell the fix worked

Save grid coordinates, query list, platform, date, and location ID for every report. Review business outcomes alongside map coverage and website clicks; update the grid only when the service area changes.

Keep a simple record for each affected URL: the problem, evidence, change, owner, and review date. This is useful when several teams touch the CMS, hosting, analytics, and content. If the same symptom returns, the record helps you spot the shared template or configuration instead of repeating one-off repairs.

## Mistakes that make this worse

Do not promise a #1 Maps position across a whole city. Do not create fake offices or use a rank grid outside an actual service area as proof of reach.

Before changing a live site, save the current configuration and test the change on a small set of representative URLs. If you are unsure whether an action removes content, changes access, or affects checkout, get the developer or site owner to review it first. Fixing the visible symptom while breaking a related page can cost more than the original search issue.

## FAQ

**Why do local rankings change when I move one street?**

Local results can respond to the searcher’s location. Grid tracking samples this variation rather than eliminating it.
