**Short answer:** A small business can earn useful backlinks by creating something worth referencing, contributing real expertise, supporting local organisations and asking partners to link to the right page. Avoid buying links, mass directory submissions and fake guest posts designed only to change rankings.

![A link-building plan focused on useful resources, genuine partnerships and real expertise](/site/blog/ethical-backlinks.svg)

## What a backlink can do

A backlink is a link from another website to yours. It may send visitors and help search engines find pages. A relevant mention from a local chamber, supplier or community group can be useful even when it sends only a few visitors.

A link is not a vote that automatically improves rankings. Google’s spam policies prohibit link schemes intended to manipulate search results.

## Start with people who already know your work

Make a list of genuine relationships:

- Suppliers and professional partners.
- Trade associations or chambers you belong to.
- Local events, charities or community projects you support.
- Customers or publications that have permission to feature your work.
- Schools, universities or training groups where you provide real expertise.

A mention should be accurate and editorially useful. Do not pay for a followed link or agree to a link exchange just to influence ranking.

## Create one useful thing to reference

Examples include a local price guide, a checklist, a clear explanation of regulations by a qualified expert, original project photographs, or a resource for customers choosing a service. Make it genuinely helpful, then share it with people who would reasonably use it.

Do not publish fake studies, invented statistics or AI-written guest posts sent to hundreds of websites. A small number of real relationships is safer and more valuable than a large list of unrelated sites.

## Ask without pressure

A simple outreach message might say:

> We created a checklist for homeowners preparing for a kitchen renovation. It explains the questions to ask before booking work. If it is useful for your renovation resources page, here is the link. No worries if it does not fit.

The recipient decides whether it belongs on their site. Do not demand exact-match anchor text or a followed link.

## Track the business outcome

Record the source, referral visits, enquiries and relationship. Do not judge success only by a tool’s authority score. If a directory sends no relevant customers, is inaccurate or asks for payment for ranking links, it may not be worth the effort.

## A local outreach example

A neighbourhood bicycle shop runs a free monthly bike-safety check with a community group. The group publishes an event page and links to the shop's safety checklist because participants need it. The link is relevant because the resource supports the event, not because the shop asked for a ranking boost.

A useful note to a partner can be brief: explain what you made, who it helps and why it may fit their page. Do not ask them to hide a paid relationship, use a specific search phrase as anchor text or link to every partner in return.

Review links periodically for referral visits and business relationships. If someone asks for payment for a followed link, a guaranteed ranking position or a large network of unrelated links, decline and spend that time on a useful customer resource instead.

## Check whether a link source is worth your time

Before asking for a link, open the page and ask whether a real customer might visit it. Is the organisation connected to your field, location or audience? Does it publish useful information and have a real editorial purpose? If the only reason the site exists is to sell links, skip it.

Keep an outreach record with who you contacted, why the resource fits, and whether they replied. Do not send automated messages to hundreds of unrelated sites. A polite note to a relevant partner protects the business’s reputation even if they choose not to link.

For a new business, there may be only a few sensible opportunities. That is normal. Build a helpful resource, serve customers well and earn mentions over time rather than buying a large package of unknown links.

## A low-cost first month

Choose one useful resource customers already need and improve it. Share it with a small group of real partners who can help it reach the right audience. Ask for feedback before asking for a link. If the resource is not useful enough for a partner to share, make it more practical rather than sending more messages.

You can also look for mentions of your business that do not link to your site. Contact the publisher politely, confirm the business details and ask whether a link would help readers find the official page. They are free to decline.

## FAQ

**Should a new business buy backlinks?**

No. Paid links intended to manipulate rankings can violate Google’s spam policies and waste money.

**Are all directories bad?**

No. A legitimate, relevant directory can help people find a business. Avoid mass submissions to low-quality sites.

**How many links do I need?**

There is no universal target. Focus on relevant visitors, trusted mentions and a useful site.
