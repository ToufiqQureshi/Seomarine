**Short answer:** A useful 30-day SEO plan does not try to “do everything.” First fix anything stopping search engines or customers from using the site. Then improve one page tied to a real business goal, make the business information trustworthy, and set up a simple way to measure enquiries. The plan below assumes a few focused hours each week.

![A four-week SEO plan moving from technical checks to one improved page, local proof and measurement](/site/blog/30-day-seo-plan.svg)

## Before you start: choose one outcome

Pick a business result you can observe: more qualified calls, quote requests, bookings or sales for a specific service. “Rank higher” is not a business outcome by itself.

Write down:

- The service or product you want to grow.
- The customer and area you want to reach.
- The page that should earn that enquiry.
- Your current monthly enquiries from that page, if known.

If you are unsure which search terms matter, use our guide to [research local keywords by customer intent](/blog/keyword-research-for-local-businesses). Choose a realistic page to improve; a new blog post is not always the best answer.

## Week 1: remove blockers and get a baseline

Spend the first week checking that important pages can be found and used.

1. Open your site on a phone. Test the main call, WhatsApp, booking or quote button.
2. In Google Search Console, check **Pages** for excluded or error URLs that should be indexed. Inspect your main service page.
3. Check that the page loads without a login, returns the expected status, and is not blocked by a noindex tag or robots rule.
4. Make sure the sitemap contains canonical pages that return successfully. A sitemap helps discovery; it does not force Google to index every URL.
5. Record a baseline: Search Console clicks and impressions for the page, plus calls, forms or bookings from your analytics/CRM.

Fix urgent broken links, wrong redirects and missing pages first. Do not spend this week chasing tiny speed-score differences if customers cannot submit your form.

## Week 2: make one important page answer the buyer

Read the page as if you were a first-time customer. Can you quickly tell what the business does, who it serves, where it works, what happens next and how to contact it?

Add only facts you can support:

- The specific service, product or appointment offered.
- The real service area or delivery limits.
- What is included, what affects price, and how to request a quote.
- Useful evidence: original photos, process details, qualifications, examples or answers from customer conversations.
- A clear next action that works on a phone.

Use the language customers use, but do not repeat the same keyword unnaturally. One strong service page is often more useful than several near-identical city pages. Google's guidance is to make helpful, people-first content, not pages built mainly to capture search traffic: [creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

## Week 3: improve local trust and internal paths

If you serve customers in person or at their locations, check that your Google Business Profile represents the business accurately. Use the real business name, correct category, working phone and hours, and a location/service-area setup that follows Google's rules.

Ask recent customers for honest reviews without offering rewards or scripting their ratings. Reply helpfully and never disclose private customer information. Google explains its [local ranking factors](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) and [review policies](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7400114?hl=en).

On your site, link to the improved service page from relevant pages using descriptive words. Link between related services only when it helps a reader take the next step. If you have multiple locations, each page should have real, distinct information; changing only the town name creates a poor experience.

Our [small-business local SEO checklist](/blog/local-seo-checklist-for-small-businesses) covers profile, website and measurement checks in more detail.

## Week 4: measure, learn and pick the next task

Compare a full recent period with a comparable prior period. Use Search Console to examine queries and pages, then compare those clicks with recorded calls, forms or sales. Search Console clicks and Analytics visits are different measurements, so do not expect them to match exactly.

Review:

- Did the page become indexed and remain technically accessible?
- Which queries brought relevant visitors?
- Did qualified enquiries change? Check the actual leads, not just visits.
- Did the phone button, form or booking flow work on mobile?
- What question did customers still ask before buying?

If impressions rise but clicks fall, diagnose query and page segments before rewriting titles; follow our [Search Console impressions and clicks guide](/blog/search-console-impressions-clicks-down). If the page gets visits but no enquiries, inspect the offer, trust signals and contact path before publishing more articles.

## Keep the work small enough to finish

Use this weekly log:

| Week | One task | Evidence it is done | Measure to revisit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Fix one important access or conversion blocker | Page and form work on mobile | Indexed status, successful test enquiry |
| 2 | Improve one service page | Clear offer, proof and next step | Relevant query clicks and lead quality |
| 3 | Correct profile details and useful internal links | Profile and links checked | Calls, directions or enquiries |
| 4 | Review results and choose the next task | Baseline and comparison recorded | Qualified leads, not rank alone |

SEO rarely produces a reliable promise of results in 30 days. Search engines need to crawl changes, demand changes over time, and leads depend on the offer and market too. The goal of this month is a better page, fewer avoidable blockers and a clear next decision.
